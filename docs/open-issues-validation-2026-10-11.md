## Compatibility and recovery behavior

Canonical fingerprints cover backend (including actual neural library/runtime versions), immutable remote asset revision or offline content digest, dimensions, pooling, normalization, dtype, schema version, and paired query/document modes and instructions. Query and persisted memory/catalog fingerprints must match. Unknown legacy vectors remain lexical-only. Hash fallback records the actual hash identity; invalid encoder output is rejected before querying PostgreSQL. Custom asymmetric models must use their actual separate encoder methods. ReMem serializes its neural pipeline loads and restores shared loader settings on success or failure. Default pinned-remote loading cannot inherit unidentified local assets; existing external remote-download prohibitions remain effective. Consecutive/concurrent-load tests cover that boundary.

Seven real PostgreSQL tests demonstrate same-label/dimension mismatch rejection, lexical continuity, staged two-batch atomic cutover, missing/legacy vector recovery isolated to the runner's provider, separate query/document encoders, query-instruction mismatch, expired claim recovery and source-change invalidation, cancellation, inference failure/retry, failed-cutover rollback with reuse of ready stages, and preservation of source writes when optional vector SQL fails. The isolation vectors in these tests are deterministic one-hot controls; they establish database/identity contracts, not neural ranking quality.

A batch can complete while generation coverage is incomplete. `coverage.cutover: completed` requires `compatible === total` and zero pending retained sources. Memory and catalog stages promote together under a short bounded lock. Persisting the configured settings cannot claim compatible coverage. Doctor separately reports fingerprint backlog, staged vectors, claims and expired runs. The [operator guide](embedding-compatibility.md) describes upgrade/backfill and interrupted retries; [ADR 0019](adr/0019-embedding-space-and-staged-cutover.md) records the decision.

## Supported limits

Capture and evidence-admission defaults are unchanged. Pi/v1 remain conservative user-statement integrations rather than general v2 tool-evidence parity. PostgreSQL still requires 384-dimensional vectors; migration 0015 does not support arbitrary widths. Unknown legacy/custom vector spaces need identity/backfill, and old differently configured processes become lexical-only after cutover. Use a consistent intended configuration when running reindex workers. Historical stages for other targets may consume storage. Cutover has a bounded short write pause; inference holds no such lock. Filters are conservative protections rather than an arbitrary poisoning detector; evidence can expire/compact, and callbacks lost before persistence lack a durable outbox.

The two issue acceptance contracts are substantiated. The broader product vision remains incomplete: general model-inferred learning, broader host evidence outcomes, persisted learning diagnostics and catalog evolution are future development beyond these issues. No npm release, force push, test bypass, capture-default change or weakened security control occurred.
